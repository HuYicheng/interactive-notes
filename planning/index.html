接下来做什么，怎么做What Next, and How?

上一篇让厨房机器人知道了自己在哪、杯子在哪、误差有多大。现在有人对它说："把杯子 A 拿到岛台上。"这句话里没有一个数字，可机器人要把它变成每秒几十次的速度指令和每毫秒一次的关节指令。中间要做一连串决定：先干什么后干什么；底盘走哪条路，离墙多远；看清了没有，要不要再看一眼；手臂从哪边伸过去，才不碰倒旁边的杯子 B；端着一杯水能开多快；半路有人走过来怎么办；杯子不见了又怎么办。回答"接下来做什么"的叫决策，回答"怎么做到"的叫规划。这一篇先讲规划到底是在做什么，再按机器人真正用的方法一层层拆开：代价地图和格子上的搜索、高维空间里撒点、把路拉直、给路配上时间、边走边改；然后讲没把握时怎么决定，怎样用行为树把一件事串起来，怎样让程序自己排步骤，怎样把一句话变成计划，扩散策略这类学出来的技能站在哪里；最后看出了岔子谁来接，把所有方法放到一张图上，再给这台机器人搭出它的决策规划层，算一笔"拿一只杯子要多久"的账。On the previous page the kitchen robot learned where it is, where the cup is and how large the errors are. Now someone says to it: "Bring cup A to the island." There is not a single number in that sentence, yet the robot has to turn it into velocity commands tens of times a second and joint commands once every millisecond. In between it has to make a chain of decisions: what to do first and what after; which way the base should go, and how far from the walls; whether it has seen clearly, and whether to look again; which side the arm should reach in from so that it does not knock over cup B next to it; how fast it can drive while carrying a cup of water; what to do if someone comes along on the way; and what to do if the cup has disappeared. Answering "what to do next" is called decision-making; answering "how to get it done" is called planning. This page first explains what planning actually does, then takes apart, layer by layer, the methods robots really use: costmaps and searching a grid, sampling in high-dimensional spaces, straightening the path, adding time to it, and replanning as it goes. After that it covers how to decide when unsure, how a behaviour tree strings a task together, how to let a program put the steps in order itself, how to turn a sentence into a plan, and where learned skills such as diffusion policy stand. Finally it looks at who catches things when they go wrong, puts all the methods on one map, and builds this robot’s decision and planning layer, with a time budget for "how long does it take to fetch one cup?"

第 0 步 · 一句话，一串决定Step 0 · One sentence, a chain of decisions

把杯子 A 拿到岛台上Bring cup A to the island

还是那间 6 m × 4.5 m 的厨房，还是那台机器人：差速底盘、底盘前面的激光雷达、头上的深度相机、一条七个关节的 Franka Research 3 手臂，跑的是 ROS 2 Humble，底盘归 Nav2 管，手臂归 MoveIt 2 管（第 15 篇）。它停在岛台西南边的充电座上，朝东。杯子 A 在北边台面上、灶台跟前，东边 17 cm 是一只一样的杯子 B，里面都装着水。The same 6 m × 4.5 m kitchen, the same robot: a differential-drive base, a lidar on the front of the base, a depth camera on its head and a seven-joint Franka Research 3 arm, running ROS 2 Humble, with Nav2 in charge of the base and MoveIt 2 in charge of the arm (page 15). It is parked on its dock south-west of the island, facing east. Cup A is on the north counter, right in front of the hob; 17 cm to its east stands an identical cup, B. Both have water in them.

"把杯子 A 拿到岛台上"至少要拆成这样一串决定："Bring cup A to the island" has to be broken down into at least this chain of decisions:

  • 站在哪：手臂够得着、相机看得清的地方，这里选灶台前 (4.5, 3.3)，朝北，杯子在底盘中心前方 0.85 m。Where to stand: somewhere the arm can reach and the camera can see clearly. Here we pick (4.5, 3.3) in front of the hob, facing north, with the cup 0.85 m ahead of the centre of the base.
  • 走哪条路：岛台挡在中间，可以从西边绕，也可以从东边绕；哪边近、哪边宽，路离墙多远（第 ②、③ 步）。Which way to go: the island is in the way, so it can go round the west side or round the east side; which way is shorter, which is wider, and how far the path keeps from the walls (steps ② and ③).
  • 看清了没有：第 16 篇算过，在 0.8 m 外看一眼，杯子位置的 σ 约 6.28 mm，2σ 超过了夹爪 10 mm 的余量；要不要往前挪 25 cm 再看一眼（第 ⑧ 步）？Whether it has seen well enough: page 16 worked out that one look from 0.8 m away gives the cup’s position a σ of about 6.28 mm, and 2σ is more than the gripper’s 10 mm clearance; should it move 25 cm forward and look again (step ⑧)?
  • 手臂怎么伸：七个关节，台面上方还挂着吊柜，旁边有杯子 B，找一条哪儿都不碰的动作（第 ④、⑤ 步）。How the arm reaches: seven joints, a wall cabinet hanging above the counter and cup B alongside; find a motion that touches nothing at all (steps ④ and ⑤).
  • 多快：端着一杯水，加速太猛就洒了（第 ⑥ 步）。How fast: it is carrying a cup of water, and if it accelerates too hard, the water spills (step ⑥).
  • 半路出事：有人从东门走进来，过道里多了一把椅子，第一次没抓住，杯子根本不在那儿（第 ⑦、⑨、⑬ 步）。Trouble on the way: someone walks in through the east door, a chair has appeared in the aisle, the first grasp misses, the cup is not there at all (steps ⑦, ⑨ and ⑬).

这些决定不是一个程序做的。1998 年 Gat 总结当时的机器人系统：至少三个研究组各自独立地走到了同一种分法，至少六台机器人照它编过程序。这种分法是分成三层，而且分的依据很特别：看每一层的程序记不记事（Gat，1998）。最下面是控制层，紧紧连着传感器和执行器，每一拍的计算量有上限，尽量不记任何事；中间是排序层，决定此刻让下面跑哪个动作、用什么参数，它大量记着"刚才发生了什么"，但不做搜索；最上面是深思层，做规划、做搜索这些费时间的事，它记的是对未来的预测，在它想的这段时间里，下面可能已经换了好几个动作。今天这台厨房机器人几乎就是这个样子：These decisions are not made by one program. In 1998 Gat surveyed the robot systems of the time: at least three research groups had independently arrived at the same way of dividing the work, and at least six robots had been programmed with it. The division is into three layers, and the basis for it is unusual: whether each layer’s program remembers anything (Gat, 1998). At the bottom is the controller: tightly coupled to the sensors and actuators, with a bounded amount of computation per tick, and remembering as little as possible. In the middle is the sequencer, which decides which action the layer below should run at this moment, and with which parameters; it remembers a great deal about "what just happened", but does no searching. At the top is the deliberator, which does the time-consuming work such as planning and searching; what it remembers are predictions about the future, and while it is thinking, the layers below may already have gone through several actions. Today our kitchen robot looks almost exactly like this:

  • 任务层（排序）：一棵行为树，决定此刻该做哪一件事。Nav2 的行为树每 10 毫秒"打一拍"（bt_loop_duration 默认 10 ms，我们在源码里读到的），但大部分拍子什么也不改，有事发生时才换动作（第 ⑨ 步）。Task layer (sequencer): a behaviour tree that decides which thing to do right now. Nav2’s behaviour tree is "ticked" every 10 milliseconds (bt_loop_duration defaults to 10 ms; we read this in the source code), but most ticks change nothing; it switches action only when something happens (step ⑨).
  • 规划层（深思）：被叫到时才算。底盘的全局路径默认每秒重算一次；手臂的一次规划给 5 秒的期限，在这样的厨房里通常零点几秒就算完（估）（第 ③、④ 步）。Planning layer (deliberator): it computes only when called. By default the base’s global path is replanned once a second; one arm planning request is given a 5-second deadline, and in a kitchen like this one it usually finishes in a few tenths of a second (est.) (steps ③ and ④).
  • 反应层和技能（控制）：底盘的局部控制器每秒 20 次给出速度；手臂的关节控制每 1 毫秒一次；学出来的技能生成一小段动作要零点一秒左右（第 ⑦、⑫ 步）。Reactive layer and skills (controller): the base’s local controller puts out a velocity 20 times a second; the arm’s joint control runs once every 1 millisecond; a learned skill takes about a tenth of a second to generate a short chunk of motion (steps ⑦ and ⑫).

第 ① 步 · 规划是什么Step ① · What planning is

先在模型里试，再动手Try it in the model first, then act

最省事的办法是不规划：每一刻只看眼前，往目标的方向挪一步，碰到障碍就躲开。1980 年代 Khatib 把它写成了势场：目标像一个坑，把机器人往下吸；障碍像一座小山，靠得越近推得越狠；机器人沿着最陡的方向往下走（Khatib，1986）。它算得飞快，每一刻只要知道离目标和最近的障碍多远，所以至今还常被用来做最后几厘米的避让。The simplest approach is not to plan at all: at every moment the robot looks only at what is in front of it, moves a step towards the goal, and dodges any obstacle it meets. In the 1980s Khatib formalised this as a potential field: the goal is like a pit that pulls the robot down into it; each obstacle is like a small hill that pushes harder the closer the robot gets; and the robot heads downhill along the steepest slope (Khatib, 1986). It is very fast to compute, since at each moment it only needs to know how far it is from the goal and from the nearest obstacle, which is why it is still often used for avoidance over the last few centimetres.

可它有个先天的毛病。1991 年 Koren 和 Borenstein 专门列了势场法"与生俱来的局限"：会被局部极小困住，在两个靠得很近的障碍之间过不去，在障碍旁边和窄通道里来回振荡（Koren、Borenstein，1991）。放到厨房里：机器人在岛台南边 (2.5, 1.2)，要去岛台北边 (2.5, 3.3) 放杯子。目标就在正北，岛台挡在中间；吸力朝北，岛台的推力朝南，走到岛台跟前两个力正好抵消，它就停在那里，离目标还有 2 米。再比如抓杯子的位置离台面只有 0.6 m（车边离台面 0.3 m），台面的推力也够得着，它会在离目标 0.12 m 的地方停下来（示意的参数，我们算的）。But it has a built-in flaw. In 1991 Koren and Borenstein devoted a paper to the "inherent limitations" of potential-field methods: they get trapped in local minima, cannot pass between two obstacles that are close together, and oscillate next to obstacles and in narrow passages (Koren and Borenstein, 1991). In the kitchen: the robot is south of the island at (2.5, 1.2) and has to put the cup down north of the island, at (2.5, 3.3). The goal is due north, with the island in the way; the pull points north, the island’s push points south, and right in front of the island the two forces cancel exactly, so the robot stops there, still 2 m from the goal. Or take the grasp pose, only 0.6 m from the counter (the robot’s edge is 0.3 m from it): the counter’s push reaches that far too, and the robot stops 0.12 m short of the goal (illustrative parameters; our arithmetic).

问题出在它只看一步。要知道"往北走不通、得先往西绕"，就得在动手之前，在脑子里把好几种走法都试一遍。这个脑子里的东西叫模型：做了这个动作，世界会变成什么样。规划就是在模型里搜索：从现在的状态出发，试各种动作序列，找一条既满足约束、代价又小的。The trouble is that it only looks one step ahead. To know that "north is blocked, so go round to the west first", the robot has to try out several routes in its head before it acts. This thing in its head is called a model: what the world will become after a given action. Planning is search in the model: start from the current state, try different sequences of actions, and find one that both satisfies the constraints and has a low cost.

minu0, …, uT−1 Σt c(xt, ut)xt+1 = f(xt, ut),  xt ∈ Xfree,  xT ∈ Xgoal

找一串动作 u0 到 uT−1，让每一步的代价加起来最小；条件是：状态按模型 f 一步步往前推，每一步都不能进到障碍里，最后要落在目标区域里。Find a sequence of actions u0 to uT−1 that makes the step costs add up to as little as possible, subject to these conditions: the state moves forward one step at a time according to the model f, no step may enter an obstacle, and the last state must land in the goal region.

几乎所有规划问题都能写成这个样子，区别只在 x、u、f、c 是什么。底盘在格子地图上：x 是一个格子，u 是往周围 8 个格子里的一个走一步，f 就是"走过去"，c 是这一步的长度乘上格子的价钱（第 ②、③ 步）。手臂：x 是七个关节角，u 是一小段转动，Xfree 是不碰任何东西的那些姿态（第 ④ 步）。任务：x 是一组事实，比如"杯子 A 在台面上"，u 是"拿起"、"放下"这样的动作（第 ⑩ 步）。第 6 篇的 MPC 和第 8 篇用世界模型做规划，也是这一行字，只是 f 换成了动力学或者学出来的网络。Almost every planning problem can be written in this form; the only difference is what x, u, f and c are. For the base on a grid map: x is a cell, u is a step into one of the 8 neighbouring cells, f is simply "move there", and c is the length of the step times the cell’s price (steps ② and ③). For the arm: x is the seven joint angles, u is a small rotation, and Xfree is the set of poses that touch nothing (step ④). For a task: x is a set of facts such as "cup A is on the counter", and u is an action such as "pick up" or "put down" (step ⑩). The MPC of page 6 and the planning with a world model on page 8 are this same one line as well, only with f replaced by the dynamics or by a learned network.

Looking just 20 steps ahead already gives 820 ≈ 1.2 × 1018 ways to go, far too many to try one by one; the A* of step ③ found the best one after expanding only 1493 cells (our arithmetic).

规划能做的，最多和模型一样好：模型里没有的椅子，规划不会绕开它；模型错了，计划也跟着错。所以规划出来的东西总要边做边看、随时重算（第 ⑦、⑬ 步）。Planning is at best as good as its model: it will not steer round a chair that is not in the model, and if the model is wrong, the plan is wrong with it. So a plan always has to be checked as it is carried out, and recomputed at any time (steps ⑦ and ⑬).

这个问题在一般情况下难得惊人。1979 年 Reif 证明，"搬钢琴"这类问题（一个由很多块组成的东西，在障碍之间从一个位置挪到另一个位置）是 PSPACE 难的：维数一多，没有已知的办法能又快又保证找到解（Reif，1979）。所以机器人上用的规划器都在某个地方让了一步：把空间切成格子，只保证在这个分辨率下找得到（第 ③ 步）；随机撒点，点撒得越多越可能找到（第 ④ 步）；从一个初值出发往好的方向改，只保证找到附近最好的（第 ⑤ 步）；或者干脆事先从示范里学好，现场不再搜（第 ⑫ 步）。规划器有两个常说的性质：完备，有路就一定在有限时间里找到，没路就能说"没路"；最优，找到的就是代价最小的那条。后面每种方法都会说清楚它在这两点上让了多少。In general this problem is astonishingly hard. In 1979 Reif proved that "piano movers’" problems (moving an object made of many parts from one position to another among obstacles) are PSPACE-hard: once there are many dimensions, no known method is both fast and guaranteed to find a solution (Reif, 1979). So every planner used on robots gives ground somewhere. Some cut space into cells and guarantee to find a path only at that resolution (step ③); some scatter random samples, and the more samples they scatter, the more likely they are to find one (step ④); some start from an initial guess and improve it, guaranteeing only the best solution nearby (step ⑤); and some simply learn from demonstrations in advance and do no search on the spot (step ⑫). Two properties of planners come up again and again: complete, meaning that if a path exists it is always found in finite time, and if there is none the planner can say "no path"; and optimal, meaning that the path it finds is the one with the lowest cost. For each method that follows we will spell out how much ground it gives on these two.

Planning is search in a model before acting: given the states, actions, model, cost and constraints, find the best sequence of actions. 第 ② 步 · 代价地图Step ② · Costmaps

把机器人缩成一个点Shrink the robot to a point

机器人不是一个点，它是一个半径 0.30 m 的圆盘。要判断"底盘中心在这里会不会撞"，得查以这一点为圆心、0.30 m 为半径的整个圆里有没有障碍，每走一步都这样查就太费事了。1979 年 Lozano-Pérez 和 Wesley 用了一个漂亮的换法：反过来把每个障碍都向外胀出一个机器人的大小，然后把机器人当成一个点，只要这个点不进到胀大了的障碍里，原来的圆盘就不会碰到原来的障碍（Lozano-Pérez、Wesley，1979）。1983 年 Lozano-Pérez 把它推广到会转的物体和手臂，这个"机器人缩成一个点以后"的空间叫构型空间（Lozano-Pérez，1983）。圆盘机器人转身不改变形状，所以只看 x、y 就够；长方形的底盘就得把朝向 θ 也算进去，构型空间变成三维。The robot is not a point: it is a disc of radius 0.30 m. To decide "would the base collide with its centre here?", you have to check the whole circle of radius 0.30 m around that point for obstacles, and doing that at every step is a lot of work. In 1979 Lozano-Pérez and Wesley used an elegant trick: turn the problem round, grow every obstacle outwards by the size of the robot, and then treat the robot as a point. As long as the point stays out of the grown obstacles, the original disc does not touch the original obstacles (Lozano-Pérez and Wesley, 1979). In 1983 Lozano-Pérez generalised this to objects that rotate and to arms; this space "after the robot has been shrunk to a point" is called configuration space (Lozano-Pérez, 1983). A disc-shaped robot does not change shape when it turns, so x and y are enough; for a rectangular base the heading θ has to be included as well, and the configuration space becomes three-dimensional.

Nav2 的代价地图就建立在这个想法上（第 15 篇第 ⑩ 步提过它的几层）。地图切成 5 cm 的格子，每格存一个 0 到 255 的数：静态层放建图时就有的墙和家具，障碍层放激光和深度相机此刻看到的东西，膨胀层按"离最近的障碍有多远"给周围的格子标价。膨胀层不只是"胀出一个机器人的大小"这么一刀切：离障碍不到一个机器人半径的格子标 253，意思是"底盘中心到这里一定撞"；再往外一段，价钱随距离指数下降；超过 inflation_radius 就是 0。Nav2’s costmap is built on this idea (step ⑩ of page 15 mentioned its layers). The map is cut into 5 cm cells, and each cell stores a number from 0 to 255: the static layer holds the walls and furniture that were already there when the map was built, the obstacle layer holds what the lidar and the depth camera see right now, and the inflation layer puts a price on the surrounding cells according to "how far is the nearest obstacle". The inflation layer is more than a blunt, all-or-nothing "grow by the size of one robot": cells less than one robot radius from an obstacle are marked 253, meaning "the base’s centre is certain to collide here"; a little further out, the price falls exponentially with distance; beyond inflation_radius it is 0.

一个格子离最近的障碍 d 米。障碍本身标 254；比机器人半径 r 还近，标 253；在 r 和膨胀半径 R 之间，从 252 开始按指数往下掉，k 越大掉得越快；比 R 还远，不收钱。A cell is d metres from the nearest obstacle. The obstacle itself is marked 254; closer than the robot radius r, 253; between r and the inflation radius R, the price starts at 252 and falls exponentially, the faster the larger k is; further than R, there is no charge.

253 和 254（253 是"中心在这里必撞"，254 是障碍本身）规划器一律不走；255 是"没见过"，NavFn 默认（allow_unknown: true）当它能走，只是很贵。0 到 252 之间是"能走，但贵"：离墙近一点没有错，可估计有误差（第 16 篇里定位的 σ 是几厘米），贴着墙走，误差一大就蹭上了；所以宁可多走两步，也尽量走在中间。指数衰减让路自然地往通道中线靠，在窄的地方又能挤过去。全局规划器 NavFn 再把格子的价钱换成走一格的代价：50 + 0.8 × c，50 是一个空格子的价钱（我们在源码里读到的）。Planners never enter 253 or 254 (253 is "the centre is certain to collide here", 254 is the obstacle itself); 255 means "never seen", and NavFn by default (allow_unknown: true) treats it as passable, only very expensive. Anything from 0 to 252 is "passable, but expensive": being a little close to a wall is not wrong in itself, but estimates have errors (on page 16 the localisation σ was a few centimetres), and a robot that hugs the wall scrapes it as soon as the error grows a little; so it is better to take a few extra steps and keep to the middle where possible. The exponential decay draws the path naturally towards the centre line of a passage, yet still lets it squeeze through narrow places. The global planner NavFn then turns each cell’s price into the cost of moving one cell: 50 + 0.8 × c, where 50 is the price of an empty cell (we read this in the source code).

第 ③ 步 · 在格子上搜Step ③ · Searching a grid

Dijkstra 和 A*：先往哪边找Dijkstra and A*: which way to search first

有了代价地图，底盘的规划就成了图上的最短路问题：每个能走的格子是一个节点，和周围 8 个格子相连，走一步的代价是步长乘上那一格的价钱。1959 年 Dijkstra 给出的办法是：从起点出发，每次挑"从起点过来花得最少"的那个格子展开，看它的邻居能不能因此更便宜，一圈圈往外推，推到终点为止（Dijkstra，1959）。它一定能找到最便宜的路，但它对终点在哪毫不关心，往背离终点的方向也推得一样卖力。With a costmap, planning for the base becomes a shortest-path problem on a graph: each cell the base can drive on is a node, connected to the 8 cells around it, and the cost of a step is the step length times that cell’s price. The method Dijkstra gave in 1959 is this: begin at the start, and each time pick the cell that is "cheapest to reach from the start", expand it and see whether that makes any of its neighbours cheaper, pushing outwards ring by ring until you reach the goal (Dijkstra, 1959). It is certain to find the cheapest path, but it does not care at all where the goal is, and pushes just as hard in the direction away from it.

1968 年，斯坦福研究所为移动机器人 Shakey 找路，Hart、Nilsson 和 Raphael 加了一样东西：对"从这里到终点还要花多少"的估计，叫启发函数（Hart 等，1968；IEEE 里程碑）。每次挑的不再是"已经花得最少"的格子，而是"已经花的加上估计还要花的"最少的格子。这就是 A*。In 1968, while finding paths for the mobile robot Shakey at the Stanford Research Institute, Hart, Nilsson and Raphael added one thing: an estimate of "how much more it will cost to get from here to the goal", called a heuristic (Hart et al., 1968; IEEE Milestone). The cell picked each time is no longer the one that has "cost least so far", but the one with the smallest "cost so far plus estimated cost still to come". That is A*.

f(n) = g(n) + h(n)h(n) ≤ h*(n)  ⇒  g(nG) = h*(nS)

一个格子 n 的分数，是从起点走到它已经花掉的代价 g，加上估计从它到终点还要花的代价 h。每次展开分数最低的格子。只要 h 从不高估真实的剩余代价，终点第一次被挑出来展开时，那条路就是最便宜的：这时它的 g，正好等于从起点出发真实的最小代价。The score of a cell n is the cost g already spent getting there from the start, plus the estimated cost h still to come from there to the goal. Each time, expand the cell with the lowest score. As long as h never overestimates the true remaining cost, the first time the goal is picked for expansion, the path to it is the cheapest: at that moment its g equals exactly the true minimum cost from the start.

h = 0 时，A* 就是 Dijkstra：一视同仁地往四周推。h 越接近真实的剩余代价，越少走冤枉路：背离终点的格子 h 大，排在后面，往往根本轮不到。为什么不能高估？如果 h 把某条好路估得太贵，A* 可能在它之前先展开到终点，交出一条差一点的路。不高估的启发函数叫可采纳的。在代价地图上，每一格至少花 50，所以"直线距离（以格子计）× 50"永远不会高估。（严格说，像这里这样展开过的格子不再展开，还要 h 满足 h(n) ≤ 走到邻格 n′ 的代价 + h(n′)；直线距离 × 50 也满足。）With h = 0, A* is Dijkstra: it pushes outwards evenly in every direction. The closer h is to the true remaining cost, the fewer wasted detours: cells lying away from the goal have a large h, so they sit further back in the queue and often never get a turn at all. Why must it not overestimate? If h prices some good path too high, A* may expand its way to the goal before it gets to that path, and hand back a somewhat worse one. A heuristic that never overestimates is called admissible. On the costmap every cell costs at least 50, so "straight-line distance (in cells) × 50" can never overestimate. (Strictly, when expanded cells are never expanded again, as here, h must also satisfy h(n) ≤ the cost of the step to a neighbouring cell n′ + h(n′); straight-line distance × 50 satisfies this too.)

"最短"要看用什么量。按米算最短的路，往往是贴着墙的路；规划器真正最小化的是代价，代价里已经把"离墙太近"算成了钱。加权 A* 用一点最优换来大把速度，它交出的路最多贵到最优的 ε 倍，很多实际系统就是这么取舍的。"Shortest" depends on what you measure. The path that is shortest in metres is often the one hugging the wall; what the planner really minimises is cost, and the cost has already put a price on "too close to the wall". Weighted A* gives up a little optimality for a lot of speed: the path it returns costs at most ε times the optimum, and many real systems make exactly this trade-off.

Nav2 在 Humble 上默认的全局规划器是 NavFn，参数 use_astar 默认是 false，也就是 Dijkstra（我们在源码里读到的）。厨房的地图只有 120 × 90 格，Dijkstra 多展开几千个格子也花不了多少时间，默认每秒重算一次，算得起。另一组规划器 Smac 都基于 A*：2D 版本是"考虑代价的 A*"，8 邻接，适合圆形的差速底盘；Hybrid-A* 版本来自斯坦福 2008 年为无人车写的做法，搜索时把朝向也算进去，交出的路车子真的开得出来（汽车不能原地转身）；State Lattice 版本用事先算好的一组短轨迹拼路，适合形状不规则的底盘（Nav2 Smac 说明；Macenski 等，2024）。On Humble, Nav2’s default global planner is NavFn, whose parameter use_astar defaults to false, which means Dijkstra (we read this in the source code). The kitchen map is only 120 × 90 cells, so even a few thousand extra cells expanded by Dijkstra take little time, and recomputing once a second, the default, is affordable. The other set of planners, Smac, are all based on A*: the 2D version is a "cost-aware A*", 8-connected, suited to a round differential-drive base; the Hybrid-A* version comes from the method Stanford developed for a driverless car in 2008 and includes heading in the search, so the paths it returns are ones a car can really drive (a car cannot turn on the spot); the State Lattice version builds paths from a set of short trajectories computed in advance, and suits bases of irregular shape (Nav2 Smac README; Macenski et al., 2024).

格子上的搜索是分辨率完备的：在 5 cm 的格子上有路，它就找得到；比 5 cm 还窄的缝它看不见。它的代价是维数：底盘的 x、y 是两维，加上朝向是三维，再多就装不下了，这正是手臂要换办法的原因。Search on a grid is resolution-complete: if there is a path on the 5 cm grid, it will find it; a gap narrower than 5 cm it cannot see. The price is the number of dimensions: the base’s x and y make two, adding heading makes three, and beyond that the grid no longer fits, which is exactly why the arm needs a different method.

第 ④ 步 · 撒点Step ④ · Sampling

高维空间里怎么找路How to find a path in a high-dimensional space

手臂要从收起的姿态伸到杯子 A 跟前：上面挂着吊柜，前面立着一瓶酱油，旁边是杯子 B。七个关节的空间切不成格子（第 ② 步），可有一件事永远做得到：随便给一个姿态，问碰撞检测"这样撞不撞"。1990 年代的人发现，光靠这一问，在高维空间里也能找到路，办法是随机撒点。The arm has to reach from its folded pose to cup A: the wall cabinet hangs above, a bottle of soy sauce stands in front, and cup B is beside it. The space of seven joints cannot be cut into cells (step ②), but one thing can always be done: take any pose and ask the collision checker, "Does this collide?" In the 1990s researchers found that this one question alone is enough to find paths even in high-dimensional spaces, by scattering samples at random.

  • PRM（概率路网，Kavraki 等，1996）：先在关节空间里撒几百上千个不撞的点，把彼此能直接连通（连线上一路不撞）的近邻连起来，得到一张路网；以后每来一个问题，只要把起点和终点接到路网上，再在路网上搜。路网建一次能用很多次，适合环境不变、要反复规划的场合。PRM (probabilistic roadmap, Kavraki et al., 1996): first scatter a few hundred to a thousand or more collision-free points in joint space and connect the neighbours that can be joined directly (collision-free all along the line between them), giving a roadmap; after that, each new query only needs the start and goal connected to the roadmap and a search on the roadmap. A roadmap built once can be used many times, which suits settings where the environment does not change and you plan again and again.
  • RRT（快速扩展随机树，LaValle，1998）：从起点长一棵树。每一步随机撒一个点，找树上离它最近的节点，朝它长一小段；这一段不撞就留下。随机点总是更容易落在大片没探索过的地方，所以树会被"拉"着快速铺满整个空间。RRT (rapidly-exploring random tree, LaValle, 1998): grow a tree from the start. At each step, scatter one random sample, find the node of the tree nearest to it and grow a short segment towards it; if that segment is collision-free, keep it. Random samples are always more likely to land in large unexplored regions, so the tree is "pulled" outwards and quickly spreads over the whole space.
  • RRT-Connect（Kuffner、LaValle，2000）：起点和终点各长一棵树，一棵树长出新节点后，另一棵树朝它一路贪心地长过去，两棵树一碰上就有了路。它是 MoveIt 在没指定规划器时的默认选择（第 15 篇第 ⑨ 步讲过它怎么长）。RRT-Connect (Kuffner and LaValle, 2000): grow one tree from the start and one from the goal; when one tree grows a new node, the other grows greedily all the way towards it, and as soon as the two trees meet there is a path. It is MoveIt’s default when no planner is specified (step ⑨ of page 15 showed how it grows).
  • RRT*（Karaman、Frazzoli，2011）：每加一个新节点，都在它周围一圈节点里重新挑"走到这里最便宜"的父节点，再看能不能让邻居改经过它变得更便宜（重新连线）。点越撒越多，路就越来越短。RRT* (Karaman and Frazzoli, 2011): each time a new node is added, it picks afresh, from the nodes in a circle around it, the parent that makes it "cheapest to get here", then checks whether any of those neighbours would become cheaper by going through it instead (rewiring). The more samples you scatter, the shorter the path gets.

撒点的方法不保证一定找到路，但保证概率完备：只要有路，撒的点越多，找不到的概率越接近 0。在最优这一点上，Karaman 和 Frazzoli 证明了一个让人意外的结论：普通的 RRT 几乎必然收敛到一条不是最优的路，点撒得再多也收敛不到最优；RRT* 才是渐近最优的。MoveIt 的选择是先用 RRT-Connect 快速找到一条能走的路，再随机地"抄近路"，把能直接连通的两点之间的弯路删掉。Sampling methods cannot guarantee to find a path, but they are guaranteed to be probabilistically complete: if a path exists, the more samples you scatter, the closer the chance of not finding it gets to 0. On optimality, Karaman and Frazzoli proved a surprising result: plain RRT almost surely converges to a path that is not optimal, and however many samples you scatter it never converges to the optimum; only RRT* is asymptotically optimal. MoveIt’s choice is to find a feasible path quickly with RRT-Connect, then "shortcut" it at random, cutting out the detours between pairs of points that can be joined directly.

撒点最怕窄通道：空间里又窄又长的一段，整个空间里只占一点点体积，随机点很少落进去，可路偏偏必须从那里过。手臂伸到吊柜下面去够杯子，就是一个窄通道：吊柜越低，能走的那一条缝越细。What sampling fears most is a narrow passage: a long, thin stretch of the space that takes up only a tiny fraction of its volume, so random samples rarely land in it, and yet the path has to go through it. The arm reaching under the wall cabinet for the cup is a narrow passage: the lower the cabinet, the thinner the one gap it can pass through.

Pn = 1 − (1 − p)nPn ≥ 95%  ⇐  n ≥ 3 / p

窄通道占整个空间的比例是 p。撒 n 个点，至少有一个落进窄通道的概率是 1 减去"n 个点全都落在外面"的概率。要有 95% 的把握，大约要撒 3 / p 个点。The narrow passage takes up a fraction p of the whole space. Scatter n samples, and the probability that at least one lands in the passage is 1 minus the probability that "all n land outside it". To be 95% sure, you need about 3 / p samples.

每个点落在通道外的概率是 1 − p，n 个点互相独立，全落在外面的概率就是 (1 − p)n。p 很小时，(1 − p)n ≈ e−pn，令它等于 5%，得 pn ≈ 3（因为 e−3 ≈ 0.05）。所以撒点的花销和 1 / p 成正比。而 p 在高维空间里小得惊人：一条通道只要在三个关节的方向上各只有整个范围的 1/10 宽，p 就只有 10−3，至少要撒约 3000 个点，每个点都要做碰撞检测，连线上还要每隔一小段查一次。Each sample lands outside the passage with probability 1 − p; the n samples are independent, so the probability that they all land outside is (1 − p)n. When p is small, (1 − p)n ≈ e−pn; setting this equal to 5% gives pn ≈ 3 (because e−3 ≈ 0.05). So the effort of sampling grows in proportion to 1 / p. And in a high-dimensional space p is astonishingly small: it only takes a passage that is 1/10 of the full range wide along each of three joints for p to be just 10−3, and then you need at least about 3000 samples, each needing a collision check, with further checks every short interval along each connecting line.

撒点规划器失败，常常不是因为没有路，而是因为路太窄。对付窄通道的办法有：在障碍边上多撒一点、从窄通道里面也长一棵树（RRT-Connect 从终点那头长，而终点就在吊柜下面），或者先用别的办法给一个大致的初值，再交给优化去修（第 ⑤ 步）。OMPL 的 RRT 默认有 5% 的点直接取终点，能让树更快接上终点，却帮不了它钻过拐弯的窄缝。MoveIt 默认给一次规划 5 秒，过时就报失败（第 15 篇）。When a sampling planner fails, it is often not because there is no path but because the path is too narrow. Ways to deal with narrow passages: scatter more samples near obstacles; grow a tree from inside the narrow passage as well (RRT-Connect grows one from the goal end, and the goal is right under the wall cabinet); or get a rough initial guess some other way and hand it to optimisation to fix (step ⑤). By default OMPL’s RRT takes the goal itself as 5% of its samples, which helps the tree join up with the goal sooner but cannot help it squeeze through a narrow gap with a bend in it. By default MoveIt gives one planning attempt 5 s and reports failure when the time runs out (page 15).

每一步长多远也有讲究。OMPL 默认的一步是整个空间最大跨度的 0.2 倍（我们在源码里读到的）：太短，树长得慢；太长，每一步都容易撞上而被扔掉。撒点的方法还有一个代价：同一个问题，每次规划出来的路都不一样，弯弯绕绕，所以后面必须平滑（第 ⑤ 步），也很难事先证明"这个动作一定安全"。How far each step grows matters too. OMPL’s default step is 0.2 times the largest extent of the whole space (we read this in the source code): too short and the tree grows slowly; too long and every step is likely to hit something and be thrown away. Sampling has one more cost: for the same problem, the path comes out different every time, twisting and winding, so it must be smoothed afterwards (step ⑤), and it is hard to prove in advance that "this motion is certainly safe".

第 ⑤ 步 · 拉直Step ⑤ · Straightening

轨迹优化：从一条路出发，往好的方向改Trajectory optimisation: start from a path and change it for the better

搜索和撒点都是先找到"一条能走的路"。另一种思路反过来：先随便给一条路，哪怕它直直地穿过岛台，再一点点地改它，让它既平滑又不碰东西。把路写成一串路点，给"不平滑"和"离障碍太近"各定一个罚分，问题就成了一个优化问题：调整路点，让总罚分最小。Search and sampling both begin by finding "a path that works". Another approach turns this round: start from any path at all, even one that cuts straight through the island, then change it bit by bit until it is smooth and touches nothing. Write the path as a chain of waypoints, give "not smooth" and "too close to an obstacle" a penalty each, and the problem becomes an optimisation problem: adjust the waypoints to make the total penalty as small as possible.

J(ξ) = Σt ‖ξt+1 − ξt‖2 + λ ∫ c(ξ(s)) dsξ ← ξ − η ∇J(ξ)

一条路由一串路点 ξ1、ξ2…组成。总代价是相邻路点之间距离的平方和（越大越不平滑），加上沿路每一处的障碍代价按路长累加起来，乘上权重 λ。每一轮，把所有路点一起朝着让总代价下降最快的方向挪一小步。A path is a chain of waypoints, ξ1, ξ2 and so on. The total cost is the sum of the squared distances between neighbouring waypoints (the larger it is, the less smooth the path), plus the obstacle cost at every point along the path, added up over the path’s length and multiplied by a weight λ. In each round, all the waypoints move together by a small step in the direction in which the total cost falls fastest.

平方和这一项像一串橡皮筋：相邻两个点离得远、拐得急，它就大，把路往直里拉。障碍项像从障碍里往外的推力：c 在离障碍远处是 0，越靠近越大，进到障碍里面越深越大；它的梯度指向障碍，每一轮沿着梯度的反方向挪，就是"离开障碍"。障碍代价要沿着路按长度累加，不能只在路点上算：只算路点的话，路点会挤到岛台边上，中间一段直接跨过岛台。两股力平衡的地方，就是一条既平滑又不碰东西的路。CHOMP 用的就是这两项，还用障碍的距离场直接算梯度，并把每一步换算成对整条路"一起挪"，避免只把某一个点推出去、路却被拉断（Ratliff 等，2009）。The sum-of-squares term is like a string of rubber bands: when neighbouring points are far apart or the path bends sharply, it is large, and it pulls the path straight. The obstacle term is like a push outwards from the obstacles: c is 0 far from an obstacle, gets larger the closer you get, and larger still the deeper you go inside one. Its gradient points towards the obstacle, so moving against the gradient each round means "moving away from the obstacle". The obstacle cost has to be added up along the path by length, not just evaluated at the waypoints: count only the waypoints and they crowd against the edges of the island while the stretch between them jumps straight across it. Where the two forces balance, you have a path that is smooth and touches nothing. CHOMP uses exactly these two terms. It also computes the gradient directly from a distance field of the obstacles, and turns each step into a move of the whole path "together", rather than pushing one point out and tearing the path apart (Ratliff et al., 2009).

梯度只知道附近哪边更好，不知道远处还有更好的。路从岛台哪一侧过，在优化开始的那一刻就由初值决定了：要看初值从哪里穿过、或者从哪边绕过岛台，而不是往哪边偏了几厘米；之后再怎么迭代也翻不过岛台去。这和第 ① 步的势场是同一个毛病：都只看局部；区别是这里改的是整条路，不是一步一步地走。所以实用的做法是：先用搜索或撒点找一条大致对的路当初值，再交给优化去拉直、拉开。The gradient only knows which way is better close by; it cannot tell that something better lies further away. Which side of the island the path takes is fixed by the initial guess the moment optimisation starts. It depends on where the guess cuts through the island, or which way round it goes, not on which way it leans by a few centimetres; however long you iterate after that, the path cannot get over the island. This is the same flaw as the potential field in step ①: both only look locally. The difference is that here the whole path is changed, rather than taking one step at a time. So the practical approach is to first find a roughly right path with search or sampling and use it as the initial guess, then hand it to the optimiser to straighten it and pull it clear.

几种常见的轨迹优化各有侧重：CHOMP（2009）用梯度，需要可导的障碍代价；STOMP（2011）不用梯度，在当前路的周围随机生成一批带噪声的路，按代价加权平均，所以代价不可导也能用（Kalakrishnan 等，2011）；TrajOpt 把问题一次次在当前解附近近似成凸问题来解，能把"不许碰撞"当成硬约束，而不只是罚分（Schulman 等，2014）。MoveIt 2 的 Humble 版带的是 OMPL、CHOMP 和工业上常用的 Pilz（直线、圆弧），TrajOpt 的代码在仓库里但默认不编译，STOMP 要到更新的版本才有（我们在源码里读到的）。The common trajectory optimisers each have a different emphasis. CHOMP (2009) uses the gradient, so it needs an obstacle cost that can be differentiated. STOMP (2011) uses no gradient: around the current path it randomly generates a batch of noisy paths and averages them, weighted by cost, so it works even when the cost cannot be differentiated (Kalakrishnan et al., 2011). TrajOpt repeatedly approximates the problem near the current solution as a convex problem and solves that, and it can treat "no collisions" as a hard constraint rather than just a penalty (Schulman et al., 2014). MoveIt 2’s Humble release ships with OMPL, CHOMP and Pilz (straight lines and circular arcs), which is widely used in industry; TrajOpt’s code is in the repository but is not built by default, and STOMP only arrives in newer releases (we read this in the source code).

这种"在模型里往前看一段、优化一串动作"的做法，其实前面几篇都见过：第 6 篇第 ⑦ 步的 MPC 就是每个控制周期解一次很短的轨迹优化，只执行第一步再重解；MPPI 用随机采样代替梯度，和 STOMP 是一个思路。第 7 篇第 ⑪ 步的 Diffuser 也是在做轨迹优化：从噪声开始一步步去噪，生成一整条轨迹，每一步再用代价的梯度把它往好的方向推。This way of working, "look ahead a little way in the model and optimise a sequence of actions", has in fact appeared on several earlier pages. The MPC in step ⑦ of page 6 solves a very short trajectory optimisation every control cycle, executes only the first step, then solves again; MPPI replaces the gradient with random sampling, the same idea as STOMP. Diffuser, in step ⑪ of page 7, is doing trajectory optimisation too: it starts from noise and denoises step by step to produce a whole trajectory, and at each step it uses the gradient of the cost to push the trajectory in a better direction.

第 ⑥ 步 · 配上时间Step ⑥ · Adding time

多快，多猛：端着一杯水能开多快How fast, how hard: how fast can you drive carrying a cup of water?

到这里规划出来的都是路径：一串位置，没有时间。真正发给机器人的是轨迹：每一刻在哪、多快、加速度多大。把路径变成轨迹叫时间参数化，要在每个关节（或底盘）的速度、加速度限制之内，尽量走快。第 15 篇第 ⑨ 步算过最简单的情形：先以最大加速度加速，匀速，再减速，T = d / v + v / a。Everything planned so far is a path: a sequence of positions with no time in it. What is actually sent to the robot is a trajectory: where it is at each moment, how fast it is moving and how hard it is accelerating. Turning a path into a trajectory is called time parameterisation, and the aim is to go as fast as possible while staying within the velocity and acceleration limits of every joint (or of the base). Step ⑨ of page 15 worked out the simplest case, T = d / v + v / a: accelerate at the maximum acceleration, cruise at constant speed, then decelerate.

MoveIt 默认的后处理用的是 Kunz 和 Stilman 的时间最优算法（TOTG）：沿着路径，在每一点上算出速度能有多大，让整条路在速度和加速度的限制下走得最快；它要求起点和终点都是停着的，默认每 0.1 秒放一个点（Kunz、Stilman，2012；MoveIt 文档）。它只管速度和加速度，不管加加速度，也就是加速度变化得多快：加速度可以从 0 一下子跳到最大。Ruckig 是另一种算法，把加加速度也限制住，还能在运动途中随时换目标，实时算出新的轨迹（Berscheid、Kröger，2021）；Humble 的 MoveIt 里它是一个可选的后处理插件。Franka Research 3 每个关节的限制写在官方文档里：前四个关节最快 2.62 rad/s，七个关节的加速度都是 10 rad/s2，加加速度都是 5000 rad/s3（Franka 文档）。MoveIt’s default post-processing uses Kunz and Stilman’s time-optimal algorithm (TOTG): along the path, it works out how fast the motion can be at each point, so that the whole path is covered as quickly as possible within the velocity and acceleration limits. It requires the motion to start and end at rest, and by default it places one point every 0.1 s (Kunz and Stilman, 2012; MoveIt docs). It deals only with velocity and acceleration, not jerk, which is how fast the acceleration changes: the acceleration can jump from 0 to its maximum in an instant. Ruckig is another algorithm. It limits jerk as well, and it can switch to a new target at any moment during a motion and compute a new trajectory in real time (Berscheid and Kröger, 2021); in MoveIt on Humble it is an optional post-processing plugin. The limits for each joint of the Franka Research 3 are given in the official documentation: the first four joints top out at 2.62 rad/s, and all seven joints are limited to 10 rad/s2 in acceleration and 5000 rad/s3 in jerk (Franka docs).

为什么要在乎加加速度？手里端着一杯水就知道了。底盘加速时，杯子里的水面会倾斜：加速度 a 越大，水面斜得越厉害，杯壁一侧的水往上爬。Why care about jerk? Carry a cup of water and you will see. When the base accelerates, the water surface in the cup tilts: the larger the acceleration a, the steeper the tilt, and the water creeps up the wall on one side of the cup.

tan φ = a / gamax = 2 g Δh / D

杯子以加速度 a 水平加速时，水面倾斜的角度 φ 满足 tan φ = a / g。杯壁处的水面升高 (D / 2) · tan φ，它超过水面到杯口的距离 Δh，水就洒出来，所以加速度最多 2 g Δh / D。When the cup accelerates horizontally at a, the water surface tilts at an angle φ with tan φ = a / g. At the wall the surface rises by (D / 2) · tan φ; once that is more than Δh, the distance from the water surface to the rim, the water spills. So the acceleration can be at most 2 g Δh / D.

在跟着杯子一起加速的坐标系里，水除了受重力 g 往下，还受一个大小为 a、方向往后的惯性力。水面总是和"重力加惯性力"的合力垂直，所以它倾斜的角度是 arctan(a / g)。杯子半径是 D / 2，水面绕中心转了 φ，杯壁处就升高 (D / 2) · tan φ。这是加速度不变时的结果；加速度突然从 0 跳到 a，水会像钟摆一样晃过头，最多晃到这个角度的两倍。In a frame that accelerates with the cup, the water feels not only gravity g pulling down but also an inertial force of magnitude a pointing backwards. The water surface is always perpendicular to the resultant of "gravity plus inertial force", so it tilts at an angle of arctan(a / g). The cup’s radius is D / 2, and with the surface turned through φ about the centre, it rises by (D / 2) · tan φ at the wall. That is the result for a constant acceleration. If the acceleration suddenly jumps from 0 to a, the water swings past this angle like a pendulum, up to twice the angle.

局部规划和重规划：有人走过来Local planning and replanning: someone comes along

全局规划用的是整张地图：建图那天的样子，再加上激光新看到的东西。机器人沿着岛台东边的过道往北开时，有人从东门走进来，朝岛台走去，正好横穿它的路。全局规划器跟不上这个人：全局代价地图虽然也接了激光，人也会进去，可它每秒才更新一次、重算一次，算一次也要些时间，人这一秒在这里，下一秒已经走开了。可底盘每秒要给出 20 次速度指令，等不起。Global planning uses the whole map: the kitchen as it was on the day the map was built, plus whatever the lidar has newly seen. As the robot drives north along the aisle on the east side of the island, someone comes in through the east door and walks towards the island, right across its path. The global planner cannot keep up with this person. The global costmap does take in the lidar as well, and the person does show up in it, but it is updated, and the path recomputed, only once a second, and each computation takes a while too: the person is here one second and gone the next. Yet the base needs a velocity command 20 times a second, and it cannot wait.

所以导航分成两级，快慢差一个数量级。全局：Nav2 的行为树用一个每秒触发一次的 RateController，让 planner_server 在全局代价地图上重算整条路（第 ⑨ 步会看到这棵树）。局部：controller_server 每秒 20 次，在一张以机器人为中心、3 m × 3 m、每秒更新 5 次的局部代价地图上，算出此刻的速度。激光看到的人会进到两张代价地图里（全局那张每秒才更新一次），也像墙一样被膨胀（以上都是 Humble 的默认值，我们在源码里读到的）。So navigation is split into two levels whose speeds differ by an order of magnitude. Global: Nav2’s behaviour tree uses a RateController that fires once a second to have planner_server recompute the whole path on the global costmap (you will see this tree in step ⑨). Local: 20 times a second, controller_server works out the velocity for this instant on a local costmap that is centred on the robot, 3 m × 3 m and updated 5 times a second. A person seen by the lidar gets into both costmaps (the global one is updated only once a second) and is inflated just like a wall (all of these are Humble’s defaults; we read this in the source code).

Humble 默认的局部控制器是 DWB，它从 1997 年 Fox、Burgard 和 Thrun 提出的动态窗口法发展而来（Fox 等，1997）。想法是：底盘有惯性，下一瞬间能达到的速度只在当前速度附近一个小窗口里；在这个窗口里撒一批候选的 (前进速度, 转动速度)，每一组都在模型里往前模拟一小段，看会走到哪、碰不碰、离路多远，打分，挑最好的那组执行；下一拍再来一次。Humble’s default local controller is DWB, which grew out of the dynamic window approach proposed by Fox, Burgard and Thrun in 1997 (Fox et al., 1997). The idea: the base has inertia, so the velocities it can reach in the next instant lie only in a small window around its current velocity. Sample a batch of candidate (forward speed, turning speed) pairs in this window; simulate each one a short way ahead in the model to see where it ends up, whether it collides and how far it is from the path; score it, and execute the best pair. At the next tick, do it all again.

Vd = { (v, ω) : |v − vc| ≤ a Δt,  |ω − ωc| ≤ α Δt }(v, ω)* = argmin(v, ω) ∈ Vd Σi wi · Ci(v, ω)

动态窗口是这一拍里能达到的所有速度：前进速度最多比现在快或慢 aΔt，转动速度最多变 αΔt。在窗口里挑一组速度，让几个评分项按权重加起来最小。The dynamic window is every velocity the base can reach within this tick: the forward speed can be at most aΔt faster or slower than now, and the turning speed can change by at most αΔt. Within the window, pick the pair of velocities that makes the weighted sum of the critics smallest.

按动态窗口法的原意，只在窗口里挑，挑出来的速度一定是底盘下一拍做得到的，不会出现"规划说立刻掉头，电机根本转不过来"。每组速度往前模拟 sim_time 秒（原意是保持这个速度不变；DWB 默认的生成器先按加速度限制从当前速度加过去），得到一小段弧线；评分项检查这段弧线：碰不碰、终点那一格贵不贵（BaseObstacle）、离全局路远不远（PathDist）、离终点近不近（GoalDist）、朝向对不对（PathAlign、GoalAlign）、是不是在原地来回晃（Oscillation）。碰撞的弧线直接扔掉。In the dynamic window approach as first conceived, you choose only inside the window, so the velocity you pick is always one the base can actually reach by the next tick; you never get "the planner says turn round at once, but the motors simply can’t turn that fast". Each velocity pair is simulated sim_time seconds ahead (originally holding that velocity constant; DWB’s default generator first ramps up to it from the current velocity within the acceleration limits), giving a short arc. The critics check this arc: whether it collides and how expensive the cell at its end is (BaseObstacle), how far it is from the global path (PathDist), how close it gets to the goal (GoalDist), whether the heading is right (PathAlign, GoalAlign), and whether it is oscillating back and forth on the spot (Oscillation). Arcs that collide are thrown away outright.

It samples 20 values each of forward and turning speed (vx_samples, vtheta_samples), about 400 candidate arcs, each simulated 1.7 s ahead: at 0.3 m/s, that means looking 0.51 m ahead (our arithmetic). The critics’ scale: 32 for distance from the path (PathDist) and heading (PathAlign), 24 for the goal (GoalDist and GoalAlign); the source code then multiplies these by resolution × 0.5, so in effect they are 0.8 and 0.6 per cell of distance. For the collision cost, BaseObstacle, it is 0.02, multiplied by the price of the cell at the end of the arc (0 to 252); if even one cell along the arc reaches 253 or above, the whole arc is thrown away (we read this in the source code).

局部规划只看眼前 1.7 s，它绕得过一个人，绕不过一整面墙：路被彻底堵死时，它找不到一条不碰的弧线，只能发零速度停下，0.3 s 后（failure_tolerance）就向上报失败；还能走、却总也走不出去时，靠进度检查：默认 10 秒内没走出 0.5 m，就判定"卡住了"。这时要靠上一级：全局规划每秒重算一次，换一条路；还不行，就交给行为树里的恢复动作（第 ⑬ 步）。Local planning looks only at what is right in front of it, 1.7 s ahead: it can get round a person, but not round a whole wall. When the path is completely blocked, it cannot find an arc that does not collide, so all it can do is send zero velocity and stop; after 0.3 s (failure_tolerance) it reports failure upwards. When the robot can still move but never manages to get anywhere, the progress checker steps in: by default, if it has not covered 0.5 m within 10 seconds, it is judged to be "stuck". Then it is up to the level above: global planning recomputes once a second and finds another path; if that still does not work, the job is handed to the recovery behaviours in the behaviour tree (step ⑬).

从 Jazzy 起，Nav2 的默认局部控制器换成了 MPPI：不在速度窗口里撒点，而是每一拍随机生成 2000 条控制序列（Jazzy 自带参数文件里的 batch_size；这个参数本身的默认值是 1000），各往前模拟 56 步、每步 0.05 s，按代价加权平均（第 6 篇第 ⑦ 步；我们在源码里读到的）。思路一样，都是"在模型里往前看一小段，按好坏挑一条或者加权平均，只执行一拍"。这台跑 Humble 的机器人，默认还是 DWB。同样叫 Nav2，不同版本的默认插件不一样：读一台机器人时，先看它跑的是哪个版本、参数文件里写的是哪个插件。From Jazzy on, Nav2’s default local controller is MPPI. The idea is the same: "look a short way ahead in the model, pick one by how good it is or take a weighted average, and execute just one tick". This robot runs Humble, so by default it still uses DWB. It is all called Nav2, but different versions have different default plugins: when you study a robot, first check which version it runs and which plugin its parameter file names.

第 ⑧ 步 · 没把握的时候Step ⑧ · When it isn’t sure

现在抓，还是再看一眼Grasp now, or look again?

前面的规划器都默认一件事：机器人在哪、杯子在哪，是知道的。第 16 篇说过，这只是一个估计，带着误差。规划器拿到的其实是"杯子大概在这里，σ 是 6.28 mm"。这时有两种选择：现在就抓，冒一点抓空的险；或者先花几秒做一件不直接推进任务、只为了看得更清楚的事：往前挪 25 cm 再看一眼，σ 降到 4.49 mm（第 16 篇第 ⑯ 步）。哪个好？要看两样东西：各自成功的概率，和失败了要赔多少。All the planners so far take one thing for granted: that we know where the robot is and where the cup is. As page 16 said, that is only an estimate, and it comes with errors. What the planner really gets is "the cup is roughly here, and σ is 6.28 mm". There are then two choices: grasp now, and accept a small risk of missing; or first spend a few seconds on something that does not move the task forward directly but only helps the robot see more clearly: move 25 cm closer and look again, which brings σ down to 4.49 mm (step ⑯ of page 16). Which is better? That depends on two things: how likely each is to succeed, and how much you pay if it fails.

E[C(a)] = Σo P(o | a) · C(o)Pok = 2 Φ(c / σ) − 1

一个选择 a 的期望代价，等于它每一种结果 o 的代价，按这种结果出现的概率加权加起来。抓杯子时，误差在余量 ±c 以内就成功，这个概率是 2Φ(c / σ) − 1。The expected cost of a choice a is the cost of each of its outcomes o, weighted by how likely that outcome is, all added up. When grasping the cup, it succeeds if the error is within the clearance ±c; the probability of that is 2Φ(c / σ) − 1.

杯子沿夹爪合拢方向的位置误差，近似是平均值 0、标准差 σ 的高斯分布（第 16 篇）。夹爪张开 80 mm、杯子直径 60 mm，误差在 ±10 mm 以内都夹得住。高斯分布落在 ±c 以内的概率，是标准正态分布从 −c/σ 到 c/σ 的面积，也就是 2Φ(c/σ) − 1。现在抓：成功不用多花时间，失败要赔 C 秒（退回来、重新看、重新抓，甚至收拾残局）。再看一眼：先花 t 秒，然后以更高的概率成功，失败同样赔 C 秒。比较两个期望代价，选小的。The cup’s position error along the closing direction of the gripper is roughly Gaussian, with mean 0 and standard deviation σ (page 16). The gripper opens to 80 mm and the cup is 60 mm across, so any error within ±10 mm can still be gripped. The probability that a Gaussian falls within ±c is the area under the standard normal distribution from −c/σ to c/σ, which is 2Φ(c/σ) − 1. Grasp now: success costs no extra time; failure costs C seconds (back off, look afresh, grasp again, perhaps even clear up the mess). Look again: spend t seconds first, then succeed with a higher probability; failure costs the same C seconds. Compare the two expected costs and pick the smaller.

σ = 6.28 mm：P = 2Φ(1.59) − 1 ≈ 0.889；再看一眼，σ = 4.49 mm：P ≈ 0.974。再看一眼要 3 s。失败一次赔 20 s（重抓）：现在抓的期望代价 0.111 × 20 ≈ 2.2 s，再看一眼 3 + 0.026 × 20 ≈ 3.5 s，现在抓划算；失败一次赔 120 s（杯子碰倒了，要人来擦）：13.4 s 对 6.1 s，再看一眼划算。两者相等时，失败的代价 C* = 3 / (0.974 − 0.889) ≈ 35 s（我们算的）。σ = 6.28 mm: P = 2Φ(1.59) − 1 ≈ 0.889; after another look, σ = 4.49 mm: P ≈ 0.974. Looking again takes 3 s. If a failure costs 20 s (a regrasp): grasping now has an expected cost of 0.111 × 20 ≈ 2.2 s and looking again 3 + 0.026 × 20 ≈ 3.5 s, so grasping now is the better deal. If a failure costs 120 s (the cup is knocked over and a person has to wipe up): 13.4 s against 6.1 s, so looking again is the better deal. The two are equal when the failure cost is C* = 3 / (0.974 − 0.889) ≈ 35 s (our arithmetic).

同一个 σ，做不做"多看一眼"，取决于失败有多贵。所以"估计到多准才够"不是估计那一层能单独回答的问题，它要拿到决策这一层，和后果一起算。端着一杯水、旁边还有一只装满的杯子，C 就大，值得多看一眼。With the same σ, whether to take "another look" depends on how expensive failure is. So "how accurate does the estimate need to be?" is not a question the estimation layer can answer on its own: it has to be taken up to the decision-making layer and worked out together with the consequences. With a cup of water to carry and a full cup right beside it, C is large, and another look is worth it.

"再看一眼"这种动作很特别：它不让任务往前走，只是让机器人知道得更多。把它当成一种普通的选择、和别的动作一起比较，最一般的框架是部分可观的马尔可夫决策过程（POMDP）：状态看不全，规划的对象不再是"我在哪"，而是"我认为我可能在哪"的整个分布，也就是第 16 篇的置信；每个动作既会改变世界，也会改变置信（Kaelbling 等，1998）。它很优雅，可精确地解它很快就算不动了：置信是一个分布，可能的置信比可能的状态多得多。"Look again" is an unusual kind of action: it does not move the task forward; it only lets the robot know more. The most general framework that treats it as an ordinary choice, weighed alongside the other actions, is the partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP): the state cannot be seen in full, so what you plan over is no longer "where am I" but the whole distribution of "where I think I might be", that is, the belief of page 16; every action changes both the world and the belief (Kaelbling et al., 1998). It is elegant, but solving it exactly quickly becomes too much to compute: a belief is a distribution, and there are far more possible beliefs than possible states.

第 ⑨ 步 · 行为树Step ⑨ · Behaviour trees

把一件事串起来：状态机和行为树Stringing a job together: state machines and behaviour trees

规划器回答"怎么做到"，可"先做什么、做完了做什么、做砸了怎么办"得有人管。这是第 0 步说的排序层。最直接的写法是有限状态机：列出几个状态（"去灶台""看杯子""抓""去岛台""放下"），再写出状态之间的每一条切换："到了 → 看杯子""看不清 → 挪近""抓空 → 再抓""杯子不见了 → 求助"……ROS 1 时代常用的 SMACH 就是一个状态机库（Bohren、Cousins，2010）。Planners answer "how do we get it done?", but someone has to be in charge of "what to do first, what to do once that’s done, and what to do if it goes wrong". This is the sequencer from step 0. The most direct way to write one is a finite-state machine: list a few states ("go to the hob", "look at the cup", "grasp", "go to the island", "put down"), then write out every transition between them: "arrived → look at the cup", "can’t see it clearly → move closer", "missed → grasp again", "cup is gone → ask for help", and so on. SMACH, widely used in the ROS 1 days, is one such state-machine library (Bohren and Cousins, 2010).

状态机的麻烦在于切换线：每加一种出错的情况，就要在好几个状态上各加一条线，指向处理它的状态，处理完再连回去；想在另一个任务里复用"看清杯子"这一段，也得把它连出去的线一根根改掉。游戏行业先碰到了这个问题。2005 年，Bungie 的 Damian Isla 介绍《光环 2》的 AI 时，把它描述成"分层有限状态机，或者说一棵行为树"：每一层按优先级从上往下试它的孩子（Isla，2005）。后来机器人界把它整理成了一套严格的定义（Colledanchise、Ögren，2018），ROS 2 里最常用的实现是 BehaviorTree.CPP，Nav2 的导航流程就写在一棵这样的树里。The trouble with a state machine is its transitions. Each new kind of failure means adding a line on each of several states, pointing to the state that handles it, and another line back once it has been handled; and to reuse the "see the cup clearly" part in another task, you have to rewire the lines leading out of it, one by one. The games industry ran into this first. In 2005, presenting the AI of Halo 2, Bungie’s Damian Isla described it as "a hierarchical finite-state machine, or a behaviour tree": each layer tries its children from the top down, in order of priority (Isla, 2005). The robotics community later turned it into a rigorous definition (Colledanchise and Ögren, 2018); in ROS 2 the most widely used implementation is BehaviorTree.CPP, and Nav2’s navigation is itself written as one of these trees.

行为树的规则很少。根节点每隔一小段时间被"打一拍"（tick），这一拍从根往下传；每个节点被打到时，回一个状态：成功、失败，或者还在做（RUNNING）。叶子是动作（导航、抓取，可能要做好几秒，期间一直回"还在做"）和条件（"到灶台前了吗？"，立刻回成功或失败）。中间的节点决定怎样把拍子传给孩子：Behaviour trees have very few rules. At short intervals the root is given a "tick", which travels down the tree from the root; each node, when the tick reaches it, returns a status: success, failure or still running (RUNNING). The leaves are actions (navigating or grasping, which may take several seconds and keep returning "still running" the whole time) and conditions ("are we at the hob yet?", which return success or failure straight away). The nodes in between decide how to pass the tick on to their children:

Seq(c1, …, cn) = s(ck),  k = min{ i : s(ci) ≠ S }Fb(c1, …, cn) = s(ck),  k = min{ i : s(ci) ≠ F }s(ci) = S ∀ i ⇒ Seq = S,  s(ci) = F ∀ i ⇒ Fb = F

顺序节点（Sequence）从左往右打它的孩子，第一个没有成功的孩子回什么，它就回什么；全都成功，它才成功。回退节点（Fallback）反过来：第一个没有失败的孩子回什么，它就回什么；全都失败，它才失败。A Sequence node ticks its children from left to right and returns whatever the first child that has not succeeded returns; only if all of them succeed does it succeed. A Fallback node is the reverse: it returns whatever the first child that has not failed returns; only if all of them fail does it fail.

顺序节点是"一件接一件，哪件砸了就算砸了"，回退节点是"这个不行就试下一个"。两者一组合，"先试正常做法，不行就换个做法，再不行就求助"就成了一棵小树，不用画切换线：失败会沿着树自然地往上传，传到第一个回退节点为止。"还在做"也沿着树往上传，所以一拍很快就打完，长时间的动作在后台继续。BehaviorTree.CPP 第 3 版（Humble 用的）里，普通的 Sequence 在孩子失败时下一拍从头再来，孩子还在做时下一拍接着打那个孩子；ReactiveSequence 则每一拍都从第一个孩子重新查起，适合"条件一变就立刻打断"（我们在它的文档里读到的）。A Sequence node means "one thing after another, and if any one fails, the whole thing has failed"; a Fallback node means "if this doesn’t work, try the next one". Put the two together and "try the normal way first, if that fails try another way, and if that fails too, ask for help" becomes a small tree, with no transitions to draw: a failure travels up the tree on its own until it reaches the first Fallback node. "Still running" travels up the tree too, so each tick is over quickly while long actions carry on in the background. In BehaviorTree.CPP version 3 (the one Humble uses), a plain Sequence starts again from the first child on the next tick when a child fails, and when a child is still running it goes straight back to that child on the next tick; a ReactiveSequence, by contrast, checks again from the first child on every tick, which suits "interrupt the moment a condition changes" (we read this in its documentation).

行为树只是排序，它不搜索、不预测：哪一步接哪一步，是写树的人事先排好的（第 0 步说的排序层"记着过去，但不搜索"）。要让程序自己排出步骤，是下一步的符号规划。还有一点：Nav2 每 10 毫秒打一拍，可动作节点在拍子之间是在别的线程或服务器里跑的；拍子只是去问"做完没有"，不是在做事。A behaviour tree only sequences; it does not search and does not predict. Which step follows which was laid out in advance by whoever wrote the tree (the sequencer of step 0 "remembers the past, but does not search"). Getting the program to work out the steps for itself is symbolic planning, the next step. One more thing: Nav2 ticks every 10 ms, but between ticks the action nodes run in other threads or servers; a tick only asks "are you done yet?", it is not doing the work.

第 ⑩ 步 · 符号规划Step ⑩ · Symbolic planning

让程序自己排步骤Letting the program work out the steps itself

行为树里的步骤是人排好的。可要是杯子 B 正好挡在杯子 A 前面，夹爪伸不进去呢？得先把 B 挪开，放到台面另一头，再拿 A。人可以把这一支也写进树里，但情况一多，写不完。另一条路是告诉程序"世界是什么样""每个动作需要什么、会改变什么"，让它自己排出顺序。In a behaviour tree, the steps were put in order by a person. But what if cup B happens to be right in front of cup A, so the gripper can’t get in? Then B has to be moved out of the way first, to the other end of the counter, and only then can A be picked up. A person could write this branch into the tree as well, but once there are many such cases, there is no end to it. The other route is to tell the program "what the world is like" and "what each action needs and what it changes", and let it work out the order itself.

这条路和 A* 出自同一个地方。1971 年，斯坦福研究所的 Fikes 和 Nilsson 为 Shakey 写了 STRIPS：世界的状态是一组事实（"机器人在房间 1""箱子在门口"）；每个动作有前提（做它之前哪些事实必须成立）、新增（做完以后哪些事实变成真的）和删除（哪些不再成立）（Fikes、Nilsson，1971；IEEE 里程碑）。规划就成了在"事实的集合"组成的空间里搜索，和第 ③ 步在格子上搜是一回事，只是一个"格子"是世界的一种状态。1998 年，为了让各家规划器能比赛，McDermott 等人定了一种描述这类问题的语言 PDDL，一直用到今天（McDermott 等，1998）。This route comes from the same place as A*. In 1971, at the Stanford Research Institute, Fikes and Nilsson wrote STRIPS for Shakey: the state of the world is a set of facts ("the robot is in room 1", "the box is at the door"); each action has preconditions (which facts must hold before it can be done), an add list (which facts become true once it is done) and a delete list (which ones no longer hold) (Fikes and Nilsson, 1971; IEEE Milestone). Planning then becomes search in a space made of "sets of facts", the same thing as searching a grid in step ③, except that one "cell" is one state of the world. In 1998, so that planners from different groups could compete, McDermott et al. defined a language for describing such problems, PDDL, which is still in use today (McDermott et al., 1998).

a ∈ A(s)  ⇔  pre(a) ⊆ ss′ = ( s ∖ del(a) ) ∪ add(a)

一个动作 a 能在状态 s 里做，当且仅当它的前提都在 s 里。做完以后的新状态，是从 s 里删掉它的删除表，再加上它的新增表。An action a can be done in state s if and only if all of its preconditions are in s. The new state after it is done is s with the action’s delete list removed and its add list added.

事实只有"成立"和"不成立"两种，所以一个状态就是一个集合，动作就是集合运算。这样写丢掉了很多东西：杯子的精确位置、手臂的姿态、需要多久，统统不在里面；换来的是规划器能在几千、几万个状态里飞快地搜。目标也是一组事实，比如"杯子 A 在岛台上"；从初始状态出发，找到一个包含全部目标事实的状态，沿路的动作就是计划。搜索可以宽度优先，也可以像 A* 一样用启发函数，比如"目标里还有几条事实没成立"。现代的规划器（比如 Fast Downward，Helmert，2006）用的启发函数要聪明得多。A fact can only "hold" or "not hold", so a state is simply a set, and an action is a set operation. Writing things this way throws away a lot: the exact position of the cup, the pose of the arm, how long anything takes, none of it is in there. In return, the planner can search through thousands or tens of thousands of states very fast. The goal is also a set of facts, such as "cup A is on the island"; starting from the initial state, find a state that contains all the goal facts, and the actions along the way are the plan. The search can be breadth-first, or it can use a heuristic like A*, for example "how many goal facts do not hold yet". Modern planners (such as Fast Downward, Helmert, 2006) use much cleverer heuristics.

Goal: cup A is on the island. When B is not in the way, the plan is 4 steps: go to the counter, pick up A, go to the island, put down A. 符号规划只在"事实"这一层推理。"A 够不够得着"这样的事实从哪来？它其实是一个几何问题：夹爪伸进去会不会碰到 B，要问运动规划器。符号层自己不知道。Symbolic planning reasons only at the level of "facts". Where does a fact like "is A reachable?" come from? It is really a geometric question: whether the gripper would hit B on the way in is something you have to ask the motion planner. The symbolic layer does not know it on its own.

这就引出了任务与运动规划（TAMP）：符号层排步骤，几何层检查每一步做不做得到，做不到就把原因变成一条新事实，交回符号层重新排。比如先按 4 步执行，走到台面以后，到"拿起 A"时运动规划器找不到不碰 B 的抓法，于是加上"B 挡着 A"、去掉"A 够得着"，从台面前重新规划，得到后面的 5 步（整件事一共 6 步）。PDDLStream 这类方法把"找一个能放杯子的位置""找一个抓法"写成可以按需调用的采样器，让符号规划器边排边要（Garrett 等，2020）。在 ROS 2 里，PlanSys2 把 PDDL 规划器接进来，再把排出来的计划变成一棵行为树去执行（Martín 等，2021；树是怎么搭的，我们在源码里读到的）。This leads to task and motion planning (TAMP): the symbolic layer puts the steps in order, the geometric layer checks whether each step can actually be done, and when one cannot, the reason becomes a new fact that is handed back to the symbolic layer to plan again. For example, the robot starts on the 4-step plan; after it has gone to the counter and reached "pick up A", the motion planner cannot find a grasp that avoids B, so "B blocks A" is added and "A is reachable" removed, and replanning from in front of the counter gives the remaining 5 steps (6 for the whole job). Methods such as PDDLStream write "find a place where the cup can go" and "find a grasp" as samplers that can be called on demand, so the symbolic planner asks for them as it plans (Garrett et al., 2020). In ROS 2, PlanSys2 plugs a PDDL planner in and then turns the plan it produces into a behaviour tree to execute (Martín et al., 2021; how the tree is put together: we read this in the source code).

当年 Shakey 就是这样分工的：STRIPS 排步骤，一个叫 PLANEX 的程序负责执行和监控，发现执行结果和计划对不上时再重新规划（IEEE 里程碑）。五十多年过去，这个结构没怎么变。Shakey divided the work in just this way: STRIPS put the steps in order, and a program called PLANEX handled execution and monitoring, replanning when what actually happened did not match the plan (IEEE Milestone). More than fifty years on, this structure has hardly changed.

    这一步的要点：符号规划把世界写成一组事实，把动作写成前提、新增和删除，让规划器自己搜出动作顺序（STRIPS、PDDL）。它推理得快，但看不见几何：哪个杯子挡着哪个，要问运动规划器。任务与运动规划让两层来回问答：做不到的原因变成新事实，再重新规划。The point of this step: Symbolic planning writes the world as a set of facts and each action as preconditions, an add list and a delete list, and lets the planner search out the order of actions by itself (STRIPS, PDDL). It reasons fast but cannot see geometry: which cup blocks which has to be asked of the motion planner. Task and motion planning has the two layers go back and forth, asking and answering: the reason something cannot be done becomes a new fact, and the planner plans again.

    第 ⑪ 步 · 一句话Step ⑪ · One sentence

    把一句话变成计划：语言模型Turning a sentence into a plan: language models

    符号规划要人先写好事实和动作，可人说的话是"我把咖啡洒在岛台上了，帮帮我"。这里面没有一个现成的事实：咖啡要用什么擦、海绵在哪、擦完放回哪，全是常识。大语言模型恰好装着大量这样的常识，问它"洒了东西该怎么办"，它会说"找块海绵来擦"。可它不知道这台机器人此刻在哪、手里有没有东西、会不会"擦"这个动作，它说的第一步，机器人未必做得到。Symbolic planning needs someone to write down the facts and actions first, but what people actually say is "I spilled coffee on the island, can you help?" Not one ready-made fact is in there. What to wipe coffee up with, where the sponge is, where it goes back afterwards: all of that is common sense. A large language model happens to hold a great deal of exactly this kind of common sense: ask it "what should I do about a spill?" and it will say "get a sponge and wipe it up". But it does not know where this robot is at the moment, whether it is holding anything, or whether it can "wipe" at all, so the first step it suggests may be one the robot cannot do.

    2022 年 Google 的 SayCan 把这两件事分开问、再乘起来（Ahn 等，2022）。机器人有一组技能，每个技能都有一句话描述（"找一块海绵""拿起海绵"）和一个从强化学习得来的价值函数：在当前的画面下，做这个技能能成功的可能有多大（第 3 篇讲过价值函数）。每一步，语言模型给每个技能打一个"对这件事有没有用"的分，价值函数给一个"现在做不做得到"的分，两个分相乘，挑最大的那个技能去做；做完把它加到历史里，再问下一步。In 2022, Google’s SayCan asked about these two things separately and then multiplied them (Ahn et al., 2022). The robot has a set of skills. Each skill has a one-line description ("find a sponge", "pick up the sponge") and a value function learned with reinforcement learning, which says how likely the skill is to succeed given the current camera image (page 3 covered value functions). At each step the language model gives every skill a score for "is this useful for the job?" and the value function gives it a score for "can it be done now?"; the two scores are multiplied, and the skill with the largest product is carried out. Once it is done it is added to the history, and then the next step is asked for.

    π = argmaxℓ ∈ L  p(cℓ | s, ℓ) · p(ℓ | i)

    在所有技能里，挑"做得到的概率"乘"有用的概率"最大的那一个。前一个看此刻的状态，由技能自己的价值函数给出；后一个看指令和已经做过的步骤，由语言模型给出。Of all the skills, pick the one with the largest "probability it can be done" times "probability it is useful". The first depends on the current state and comes from the skill’s own value function; the second depends on the instruction and the steps already taken, and comes from the language model.

    语言模型只看文字，会推荐此刻根本做不到的事，比如机器人还在充电座边上，它就说"拿起海绵"；价值函数只看状态，不知道人要什么，只会说"去水槽是做得到的"。乘起来以后，一个技能必须两头都不低才会被选中。论文用的是 551 个技能、17 种物体，测了 101 条指令：在仿照厨房搭起来的场地里（真机器人，不是仿真），PaLM-SayCan 排对计划的比例是 84%，真正做成的是 74%；在真实的厨房里分别是 81% 和 60%。对"我洒了东西，能帮忙吗？"，它排出的计划是：找一块海绵，拿起海绵，拿给你，完成。The language model sees only text, so it will recommend things that cannot be done at the moment: with the robot still by the dock, it says "pick up the sponge". The value function sees only the state and does not know what the person wants; all it can say is "going to the sink is feasible". Once the two are multiplied, a skill is chosen only if neither score is low. The paper used 551 skills and 17 objects and tested 101 instructions. In a mock kitchen built for the tests (with real robots, not in simulation), PaLM-SayCan chose the right plan 84% of the time and actually completed the task 74% of the time; in a real kitchen the figures were 81% and 60%. For "I spilled something, can you help?", the plan it produced was: find a sponge, pick up the sponge, bring it to you, done.

    语言模型不知道物理世界，价值函数不知道人要什么；把两者乘起来，是让每一步都"既对题、又做得到"的最简单办法。但它只在给定的技能里挑，不会发明新技能；技能做砸了，它也得靠外面的监控才知道。The language model knows nothing of the physical world, and the value function knows nothing of what the person wants; multiplying the two is the simplest way to make every step "both relevant and feasible". But it only chooses among the skills it is given and never invents new ones; and when a skill goes wrong, it needs monitoring from outside to find out.

    另一种做法是让语言模型直接写程序。Code as Policies（Liang 等，2022）给代码模型看几个"指令 → 代码"的例子，它就能把新指令写成一段 Python：调用检测函数找到物体，调用运动函数让机器人动，中间用循环和条件把它们串起来；写出来的代码可以直接调用还不存在的函数；系统找出这些函数，再让模型一个个把它们写出来，写的时候又可以调用新的函数，这样递归下去。比如"看到橙子就往后退"，会写成一个 if：检测到橙子，就把前进速度设成负的。写出来的是行为树的叶子要调用的那种东西，只不过排序也交给了模型。Another approach is to have the language model write the program directly. Code as Policies (Liang et al., 2022) shows a code model a few "instruction → code" examples, and it can then write a new instruction as a piece of Python: it calls detection functions to find objects and motion functions to move the robot, and strings them together with loops and conditionals. The code it writes may call functions that do not exist yet; the system finds these functions and has the model write them one by one, and while writing them it may call new functions again, and so on, recursively. For example, "move back when you see an orange" becomes an if: if an orange is detected, set the forward velocity to a negative value. What comes out is the kind of thing a behaviour tree’s leaves call, except that the ordering is handed to the model as well.

    再往前一步，就是第 7 篇第 ⑪ 步那张表里的 VLA（RT-2、π0 这些）：把语言、画面和动作放进同一个网络，从"一句话 + 一张图"直接生成动作，规划、决策和技能被揉在了一起。它们会从技能这一层往上，把这一篇的三层吃掉多少，还是个正在发生的事。实际的系统多半还是让语言模型只决定"下一个技能"，VLA 多半当成其中一个技能来用，下面保留规划器、行为树和安全检查，因为模型的输出不保证做得到，也不保证安全。One step further are the VLAs in the table in step ⑪ of page 7 (RT-2, π0 and the like): they put language, images and actions into a single network and generate actions straight from "a sentence + an image", rolling planning, decision-making and skills into one. How much of this page’s three layers they will eat up, working upwards from the skill layer, is still playing out. Real systems mostly still have the language model decide only "the next skill" and mostly use a VLA as one of the skills, keeping planners, behaviour trees and safety checks underneath, because nothing guarantees that a model’s output is feasible, or that it is safe.

      第 ⑫ 步 · 学出来的技能Step ⑫ · Learned skills

      扩散策略站在哪Where diffusion policy sits

      到这里，规划器都是在现场"想"：手里有模型、目标、代价和约束，当场搜出一条路。还有一种完全不同的做法：让人遥控机器人把"拿起杯子"示范几十上百遍，训练一个网络，从"看到的画面"直接给出"接下来的动作"。这就是第 3 篇的模仿学习。第 7 篇第 ⑪ 步讲过它最常用的写法之一扩散策略是怎么工作的：看一眼画面，从噪声开始去噪，生成未来 16 个时刻的动作，只执行前 8 个就重新看、重新生成（Chi 等，2023）。论文里每次生成用 10 步去噪，在一块 RTX 3080 上约 0.1 s。So far, every planner has done its "thinking" on the spot: with a model, a goal, a cost and constraints in hand, it searches out a path there and then. There is a completely different approach: have a person teleoperate the robot through "pick up the cup" several dozen to a hundred or more times, and train a network that goes straight from "what it sees" to "what to do next". This is the imitation learning of page 3. Step ⑪ of page 7 explained how one of its most common forms, diffusion policy, works: take one look at the image, start from noise and denoise it into actions for the next 16 time steps, execute only the first 8, then look again and generate again (Chi et al., 2023). In the paper each generation uses 10 denoising steps and takes about 0.1 s on an RTX 3080.

      它在这一篇里站在哪？它做的正是"决定接下来怎么动"：生成一小段未来的动作，执行一部分再重想，这和第 ⑦ 步局部规划"往前看一小段、只执行一拍"是同一个节奏，所以它属于规划和决策。区别在"想"放在什么时候：Where does it sit on this page? What it does is exactly "deciding how to move next": it generates a short stretch of future actions, executes part of it and then thinks again. That is the same rhythm as the local planning of step ⑦, "look a short way ahead, execute only one tick", so it belongs to planning and decision-making. The difference lies in when the "thinking" happens:

      u* = argminu J(u; f, Xgoal, Xfree)u ∼ πθ( · | o ),   θ = argmaxθ Σk log πθ(uk | ok)

      规划器在现场求解：给定模型 f、目标和不能碰的区域，找总代价最小的一段动作。学出来的策略在现场只是抽样：从网络给出的动作分布里抽一段；网络的参数 θ 是事先用示范训练出来的，让示范里的动作在它看来可能性最大（扩散策略实际训练的是去噪的误差，相当于在近似这个目标）。A planner solves on the spot: given a model f, a goal and the regions it must not touch, it finds the stretch of actions with the lowest total cost. A learned policy only samples on the spot: it draws a stretch of actions from the distribution the network gives. The network’s parameters θ were trained beforehand on demonstrations, so that the demonstrated actions look as likely as possible to it (diffusion policy actually trains on the denoising error, which amounts to approximating this objective).

      规划器把"想"放在现场：代价是每次都要算，好处是给它一个新目标、一个新障碍、一条新约束，它马上就能照办，而且可以检查结果是否满足约束。学出来的策略把"想"提前到训练时做完，现场又快又不需要模型，可它只会做示范里那样的事：它学到的是"在这种画面下，人通常怎么动"，不知道目标和约束是什么。扩散策略比简单的回归强在一点：同一个画面下人有好几种做法时（从左边绕还是从右边绕），它只抽其中一种，不会把它们平均成一种哪边都不是的做法（第 7 篇第 ⑪ 步）。A planner does its "thinking" on the spot. The price is that it must compute every time; the benefit is that if you give it a new goal, a new obstacle or a new constraint, it can act on it straight away, and its result can be checked against the constraints. A learned policy moves the "thinking" to training time and finishes it there, so on the spot it is fast and needs no model; but it can only do what the demonstrations did. What it has learned is "with an image like this, how a person usually moves"; it does not know what the goal and the constraints are. Diffusion policy beats simple regression in one respect: when people have several ways of doing something from the same image (going round on the left or on the right), it samples just one of them instead of averaging them into a way that is neither (step ⑪ of page 7).

That is why they are often used together.

      放到这台机器人的三层里，扩散策略最自然的位置是一个技能：行为树决定什么时候调用它（先用 Nav2 开到灶台前、看清杯子，再调用"抓杯子"），它负责接触最多、最难建模的那几秒，输出的动作再交给下面的关节控制器去跟（ros2_control 每 1 毫秒一次）。不碰东西的大段运动（开过去、把手臂移到杯子附近）仍然交给规划器，因为那里有明确的目标和障碍，规划器更可靠、更容易检查。也有人把两者混起来：Diffuser 这类方法生成整条轨迹时，用代价的梯度去引导，让学出来的分布也能照顾到新的目标和障碍（第 7 篇第 ⑪ 步）。VLA 加上了语言，从一句话直接到动作，就从"技能"往上吃掉了一部分"决策"（第 ⑪ 步）。Placed in this robot’s three layers, diffusion policy fits most naturally as a skill: the behaviour tree decides when to call it (first drive to the hob with Nav2 and get a clear look at the cup, then call "grasp the cup"); it handles the few seconds with the most contact, the hardest to model, and the actions it outputs go down to the joint controllers to track (ros2_control, once every 1 ms). The long stretches of motion that touch nothing (driving over, moving the arm close to the cup) still go to the planners, because there the goals and obstacles are clear, and planners are more reliable and easier to check. Some people mix the two: methods such as Diffuser, when they generate a whole trajectory, steer it with the gradient of the cost, so that the learned distribution can also take new goals and obstacles into account (step ⑪ of page 7). A VLA adds language and goes straight from a sentence to actions, so from "skills" it eats upwards into part of "decision-making" (step ⑪).

      常有人问"扩散策略算不算规划"。可以这样回答：它是一种把规划提前做完、记在网络里的规划。它省掉了现场的搜索，也丢掉了现场搜索带来的灵活和可检查。A question people often ask is "does diffusion policy count as planning?" One way to answer: it is planning that has been done in advance and stored in a network. It saves the search on the spot, and it also gives up the flexibility and checkability that searching on the spot brings.

      第 ⑬ 步 · 出岔子Step ⑬ · When things go wrong

      出了岔子，谁来接When something goes wrong, who catches it?

      计划总会出岔子：模型里没有的东西出现了（一只伸过来的手、一把椅子），模型里的东西不在了（杯子被人拿走了），动作没做成（抓空了），甚至机器人连自己在哪都弄错了（第 16 篇第 ⑧ 步的"绑架"）。好的决策规划层不是"不出错"，而是每种岔子都有一层接得住，而且是能最快接住它的那一层先接。Plans always go wrong somewhere: something that is not in the model turns up (a hand reaching in, a chair), something in the model is no longer there (someone took the cup), an action does not work (the grasp missed), or the robot is even wrong about where it is (the "kidnapping" of step ⑧ of page 16). A good decision and planning layer is not one that "never goes wrong"; it is one where every kind of failure has a layer that can catch it, and the first to catch it is the layer that can catch it fastest.

      前面各步里已经出现过这些"接"的地方，按反应的快慢从下往上排，就是一架梯子：These places where things get "caught" have all appeared in earlier steps. Ordered by how fast they react, from the bottom up, they form a ladder:

      3. 局部控制器：每秒 20 次，绕开身边的东西（第 ⑦ 步）。Local controller: 20 times a second, steers round things nearby (step ⑦).
      4. 全局重规划：每秒 1 次，换一条路（第 ③、⑦ 步）。Global replanning: once a second, finds another route (steps ③ and ⑦).

      8. 求助：告诉人"杯子 A 不在台面上"，等人来。Ask a person: tell someone "cup A is not on the counter" and wait for them to come.

      每一层只处理自己看得见、来得及处理的事，处理不了就往上交。往上交需要两样东西：知道"出岔子了"，和知道"交给谁"。前一样叫执行监控：拿实际发生的和计划里该发生的比。Nav2 的进度检查（10 秒内没走出 0.5 m 就算卡住）、MoveIt 执行时检查起点对不对得上、是不是拖得太久，场景里多了东西还会再查剩下的路撞不撞、行为树里每个动作回的"失败"，都是执行监控。Shakey 当年专门有一个程序 PLANEX 做这件事：照着 STRIPS 排出的计划执行，发现世界和计划对不上，就判断哪些步骤还能用、要不要重新规划（IEEE 里程碑）。Each layer deals only with what it can see and has time to deal with; what it cannot handle, it passes up. Passing up needs two things: knowing that "something has gone wrong", and knowing "whom to pass it to". The first is called execution monitoring: comparing what actually happens with what the plan says should happen. Nav2’s progress checker (moving less than 0.5 m in 10 s counts as stuck); MoveIt checking during execution that the start state matches and that the motion is not taking too long, and, when something new appears in the scene, checking again whether the rest of the path collides; the "failure" that each action in a behaviour tree returns: all of these are execution monitoring. Back in its day, Shakey had a program, PLANEX, just for this: it executed the plans STRIPS produced, and when it found that the world and the plan no longer matched, it worked out which steps were still usable and whether to replan (IEEE Milestone).

      梯子最下面两层要单独说一句。它们不是规划的一部分，而是安全：不管上面的规划器、行为树、语言模型怎么想，手臂碰到人要停，底盘要撞上东西要停。手臂的碰撞反射跑在 Franka 自己的控制器里，上面全卡死了也照样管用；collision_monitor 却只是夹在速度指令上的一道过滤，每收到一条指令才查一次，上面卡死、不再发指令时，要靠底盘驱动自己的指令超时和硬件急停让它停下。Nav2 的说明里也讲得很清楚：collision_monitor 是在电脑上跑的，用的是任意传感器，它模仿的是安全激光雷达和安全控制器那一类硬件功能，但本身不提供实时的安全认证。真正对人负责的急停和限速，要由经过认证的硬件和控制器来做，规划层只是在它们之上尽量不去碰它们。The bottom two rungs of the ladder need a word of their own. They are not part of planning but of safety: whatever the planners, behaviour trees and language models above them think, the arm must stop when it touches a person, and the base must stop when it is about to hit something. The arm’s collision reflex runs in Franka’s own controller, so it still works even if everything above has frozen. By contrast, collision_monitor is only a filter on the velocity commands, which checks once each time a command arrives; if everything above freezes and stops sending commands, it is the base driver’s own command timeout and the hardware emergency stop that have to bring the robot to a halt. The Nav2 README says so plainly too: collision_monitor runs on the computer and uses arbitrary sensors; it imitates the kind of hardware function found in safety lidars and safety controllers, but it does not itself provide real-time safety certification. The emergency stops and speed limits that really protect people must come from certified hardware and controllers; the planning layer merely works above them, doing its best never to set them off.

      开头的九个问题：The nine questions from the opening:

      1. 规划到底是在做什么，为什么不能看一步走一步：在动手之前，在模型里搜一串动作，让总代价最小、又不违反约束。只看一步的势场会被局部极小困住：从岛台南边去岛台北边，它停在岛台跟前，离目标还有 2 米。一般的"搬钢琴"问题是 PSPACE 难的，所以每种实用的规划器都在完备或最优上让了一步（第 ① 步）。What is planning actually doing, and why can’t the robot just go one step at a time: before acting, it searches the model for a sequence of actions that minimises the total cost without breaking the constraints. A potential field that looks only one step ahead gets trapped in a local minimum: going from the south side of the island to the north side, it stops right in front of the island, still 2 m from the goal. The general "piano movers’ problem" is PSPACE-hard, so every practical planner gives ground on completeness or optimality (step ①).
      2. 地图上哪里能走、哪里贵，机器人的大小怎么算进去：把障碍胀出一个机器人半径，机器人就能当成一个点（构型空间）。Nav2 的膨胀层再往外按 252 · e−k(d − r) 收费，让路走在通道中间：烤箱柜前那段 1.0 m 宽的过道，中线上走一格要付空格子的 3.2 倍，所以路绕到了岛台东边。半径和膨胀要按自己的机器人设（第 ② 步）。Where on the map can the robot go and where is it expensive, and how is the robot’s size taken into account: grow the obstacles by one robot radius and the robot can be treated as a point (configuration space). Further out, Nav2’s inflation layer charges 252 · e−k(d − r), which keeps paths in the middle of passages: in the 1.0 m aisle in front of the oven cabinet, one cell on the centreline costs 3.2 times as much as an empty cell, so the path goes round the east side of the island. Set the radius and the inflation for your own robot (step ②).
      3. 在一张格子地图上，怎样又快又准地找到路：Dijkstra 一圈圈往外推，一定找到最便宜的路；A* 加上不高估的启发函数，找到一样便宜的 5.16 m 的路，只展开 1493 个格子（Dijkstra 是 3853 个）。加权 A* 和贪心用最优换速度，贪心的路贴着岛台西头挤过去，按代价算是最优的 2.8 倍。Nav2 Humble 默认的 NavFn 用的是 Dijkstra（第 ③ 步）。On a grid map, how do you find a path quickly and accurately: Dijkstra pushes outwards ring by ring and is sure to find the cheapest path; A*, adding a heuristic that never overestimates, finds an equally cheap 5.16 m path while expanding only 1493 cells (Dijkstra expands 3853). Weighted A* and greedy best-first trade optimality for speed: the greedy path squeezes tight past the west end of the island and, by cost, comes to 2.8 times the optimum. NavFn, the default in Nav2 Humble, uses Dijkstra (step ③).
      4. 手臂有七个关节，空间画不出来，怎么找一条不撞的动作：不切格子，只问"这个姿态撞不撞"，随机撒点、长树：RRT-Connect 从两头长，是 MoveIt 的默认；RRT* 不断重新连线，渐近最优。撒点是概率完备的，最怕窄通道：通道占空间的比例是 p，要撒约 3 / p 个点，才有 95% 的把握撒中一次（第 ④ 步）。The arm has seven joints and its space cannot be drawn: how do you find a motion that hits nothing: instead of cutting the space into cells, just ask "does this pose collide?", scatter random samples and grow trees. RRT-Connect grows from both ends and is MoveIt’s default; RRT* keeps rewiring and is asymptotically optimal. Sampling is probabilistically complete, and what it fears most is a narrow passage: if the passage takes up a fraction p of the space, it takes about 3 / p samples to be 95% sure of landing one in it (step ④).
      5. 找到的路歪歪扭扭，怎么拉直？配上时间时，能开多快：轨迹优化沿着"平滑"和"离障碍远"两项的梯度，一点点改整条路；它只能找到初值附近最好的那条，从岛台哪边过，开始时就定了，所以常接在搜索或撒点后面（第 ⑤ 步）。配时间，是在速度、加速度、加加速度的上限内尽量快：端着水，加速度不能超过 2gΔh/D ≈ 3.27 m/s2，也不能一下子跳上去；Nav2 默认的 2.5 m/s2 是给空着手的 TurtleBot 用的，这里设成 0.5 m/s2（第 ⑥ 步）。The path it finds is crooked: how do you straighten it? And once time is added, how fast can it go: trajectory optimisation follows the gradient of two terms, "smoothness" and "staying far from obstacles", changing the whole path a little at a time. It can only find the best path near its initial guess, and which side of the island the path takes is settled at the start, so it often runs after a search or a sampling planner (step ⑤). Adding time means going as fast as possible within the limits on velocity, acceleration and jerk: carrying water, the acceleration must not exceed 2gΔh/D ≈ 3.27 m/s2, and must not jump up all at once either; Nav2’s default of 2.5 m/s2 is meant for a TurtleBot carrying nothing, and here it is set to 0.5 m/s2 (step ⑥).
      6. 路上冒出来一个人，规划要不要从头再来：不用。全局规划每秒重算一次，看得全但慢：激光看到的人也会被标进全局代价地图，可它每秒才更新、重算一次，跟不上一个走动的人；局部规划每秒 20 次，撒约 400 条弧线，每条往前模拟 1.7 s，挑一条绕开人的。路被彻底堵死时，才交给上一级（第 ⑦ 步）。A person suddenly appears in the path: does planning have to start again from scratch: no. Global planning recomputes once a second; it sees the whole map but is slow: the person the lidar sees is marked in the global costmap too, but that map is updated, and the path recomputed, only once a second, too slow to keep up with a person walking about. Local planning runs 20 times a second, samples about 400 arcs, simulates each one 1.7 s ahead and picks one that goes round the person. Only when the way is completely blocked does it pass the problem up a level (step ⑦).
      7. 没把握的时候怎么决定，"再看一眼"值不值：按期望代价。第一次看，2σ 超过了余量，夹住的概率是 0.889；挪近再看一眼，升到 0.974，要多花 3 s。失败一次的代价超过约 35 s，就值得再看一眼。POMDP 把"看"和"做"放在一起规划，精确解算不动，实际靠留余量、勤重算和显式的"去看一眼"来近似（第 ⑧ 步）。How do you decide when you are not sure, and is "another look" worth it: by expected cost. After the first look, 2σ exceeds the clearance and the probability of gripping the cup is 0.889; moving closer and looking again raises it to 0.974, at the cost of 3 s more. If one failure costs more than about 35 s, another look is worth it. A POMDP plans "looking" and "doing" together, but exact solutions are out of reach; in practice it is approximated by leaving a margin, recomputing often and an explicit "go and take a look" (step ⑧).
      8. 一整件事怎么串起来，出了岔子谁来接：行为树排步骤：顺序节点一个个往下做，回退节点在失败时换一种做法，"还在做"让长动作在后台继续（第 ⑨ 步）；符号规划和语言模型可以在树的上面重新排步骤（第 ⑩、⑪ 步）。出了岔子，由能最快接住它的那一层先接：碰撞反射、collision_monitor、局部控制、全局重规划、Nav2 的恢复动作、任务树、重新规划，最后求助。最下面两层是安全，不属于规划（第 ⑬ 步）。How is a whole task strung together, and who catches it when something goes wrong: a behaviour tree orders the steps: a Sequence node works through its children one by one, a Fallback node tries a different way when something fails, and RUNNING ("still running") lets a long action carry on in the background (step ⑨); symbolic planning and language models can reorder the steps from above the tree (steps ⑩ and ⑪). When something goes wrong, the layer that can catch it fastest catches it first: the collision reflex, collision_monitor, local control, global replanning, Nav2’s recovery behaviours, the task tree, task replanning and, last of all, asking a person for help. The bottom two are safety, not planning (step ⑬).
      9. 一句话怎么变成计划？扩散策略这类学出来的技能放在哪：语言模型装着常识，SayCan 把"有用"和"做得到"相乘来挑技能，Code as Policies 让模型直接写调用技能的程序（第 ⑪ 步）。扩散策略属于规划和决策：它把规划提前到训练时做完，记在网络里，现场快、擅长接触，但只会示范里的事。它最自然的位置是行为树下面的一个技能，不碰东西的大段运动交给规划器（第 ⑫ 步）。How does a sentence become a plan? And where do learned skills such as diffusion policy fit: a language model holds common sense; SayCan multiplies "useful" by "feasible" to pick a skill, and Code as Policies has the model go straight to writing a program that calls the skills (step ⑪). Diffusion policy belongs to planning and decision-making: it moves the planning to training time, finishes it there and stores it in the network; at run time it is fast and good at contact, but it can only do what was in the demonstrations. Its most natural place is as a skill under the behaviour tree, with the long stretches of motion that touch nothing left to the planners (step ⑫).

      回到这几篇一直在追的目标：拿到一台陌生的机器人，能看懂、能上手，看不懂的地方知道差什么。读它的决策规划层，就按第 ⑮ 步那张表一行行对：每一层是谁、吃进什么、交出什么、多快、失败了谁接，再问那八个问题。答不上来的，多半差在这几样上：参数还是别的机器人的默认值（半径、加速度）；估计的 σ 没有进到决策里；某种失败没有人接，或者接它的那一层太慢；学出来的技能没见过眼前的情况；安全靠的是规划器，而不是经过认证的硬件。Back to the goal these pages have been chasing all along: handed an unfamiliar robot, be able to understand it and get hands-on with it, and where you cannot understand it, know what is missing. To read its decision and planning layer, go through the table in step ⑮ row by row: for each layer, who does it, what it takes in, what it hands on, how fast it runs and who catches it when it fails; then ask those eight questions. Where you cannot answer, the gap is usually one of these: the parameters are still another robot’s defaults (radius, acceleration); the σ of the estimate never makes it into the decisions; some failure has no one to catch it, or the layer that catches it is too slow; a learned skill has never seen the situation in front of it; safety relies on the planner rather than on certified hardware.
